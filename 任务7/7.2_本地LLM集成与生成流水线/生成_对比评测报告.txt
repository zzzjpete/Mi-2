**Conclusion:** Disease-modifying therapies (DMTs) for multiple sclerosis (MS) are classified based on their mechanism of action, target pathways, and route of administration. Each class has distinct pharmacological properties and clinical applications.

1. **Interferons (IFNs):**  
   - **Mechanism:** Modulate immune response by inhibiting T-cell activation and reducing pro-inflammatory cytokines.  
   - **Examples:** Interferon-beta-1a (Avonex, Rebif), Interferon-beta-1b (Betaferon, Extavia).

2. **Glatiramer Acetate (GA):**  
   - **Mechanism:** Mimics myelin basic protein and modulates T-cell response by inducing anti-inflammatory cytokines.  
   - **Example:** Copaxone.  
   - **Evidence:** GA is approved for RRMS and has been shown to reduce relapse rates in randomized controlled trials (e.g., [Comi et al., 2001](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM200103293441203)).

3. **Monoclonal Antibodies (mAbs):**  
   - **Mechanism:** Target specific immune cells or molecules involved in MS pathogenesis.  
   - **Examples:**  
     - **Ocrelizumab:** Targets CD20+ B cells (approved for RRMS and primary progressive MS).  
     - **Alemtuzumab:** Targets CD52+ lymphocytes (used in highly active RRMS).  
     - **Natalizumab:** Targets α4β1 integrin (used in RRMS and secondary progressive MS).  
     - **Daclizumab:** Targets IL-2 receptor (withdrawn due to safety concerns).  
   - **Evidence:** These agents have been evaluated in large phase III trials (e.g., [Hauser et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1013824) for ocrelizumab; [Hauser et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa060653) for natalizumab).
